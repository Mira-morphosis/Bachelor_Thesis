\chapter{Conclusions and Future work}
\label{chap:conclusions}

\noindent This chapter first summarizes the work done and its main results (Section~\ref{sec:conclusions_summary}), then discusses the impact of the contribution and its usefulness for others (Section~\ref{sec:impact}), and finally states what was not done and which developments remain open (Section~\ref{sec:future_work}).

\section{Summary of the Work and Main Results}
\label{sec:conclusions_summary}

\paragraph{What was done.} This thesis designed, implemented and preliminarily validated StARAPTOR (\emph{Steam Automated Review Association Miner for Player-count Time-series Optimized Regression}), a configurable, self-hostable and model-agnostic tool that converts unstructured Steam reviews into quantified inputs for the ML-based forecasting of the daily mean number of concurrent players over the following 30 days (Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology}). The validation (Chapter~\ref{chap:results}) targeted a single mature, high-traffic title, Cyberpunk 2077 (Steam app 1091500), over one year of data: 365 daily KPI observations and 76{,}835 fetched reviews, re-processed with zero extraction failures by the selected base model. The three studies designed in the methodology were executed under the fixed seed 42 with a recorded run manifest, so the whole validation is reproducible from the thesis branch of the project's repository: the cross-model consistency benchmark of the LLM-based extraction stage (RQ1), the pre-registered inspection of the mined association rules (RQ2) and the forecasting ablation (RQ3).

\paragraph{Main results.} Read jointly, the three studies answer the research questions as follows (full metrics are reported in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}): the LLM-based extraction stage is consistent enough to be used in analysis, although possibly game-dependently, and larger models are more consistent (RQ1); nearly three quarters of the surfaced rules are actionable, developer-facing insight, but the rule set is still noisier and more verbose than it needs to be (RQ2); and the forecasting ablation does not improve over the KPI-only baseline on the analyzed game, while reducing underestimation, so RQ3 must be read as open rather than settled (RQ3).

\paragraph{Result summary.} As described in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}, the overall picture is promising but inconsistent, and the inconsistency concentrates in the forecasting ablation, which degrades rather than improves. Inconsistency may be reduced by moving the RQ2 decision procedure into the mining filter cascade (e.g., extending entailment rule R2 to the miner itself), so that the feature matrix fed to the regressor no longer contains structural rules. However, this alone is not a complete solution: configuration C2 of the ablation already degrades the recursive 30-day MAPE by 43.7\% over the KPI-only baseline, and C3 by 51.7\%, while still cutting the underestimation rate by 69.6\%. The cause may be model-dependent (frontier or fine-tuned models could yield more informative scores; RQ1 already showed larger models to be more consistent), game-dependent (different games may be sensitive to different score consistencies), or data-dependent (short lookback windows or sparse periods weaken the baseline, and noisy review scores then cap the achievable improvement).

\section{Impact of the Work}
\label{sec:impact}

\paragraph{Usefulness for practitioners.} StARAPTOR is released as an open-source prototype (the thesis branch of the project's repository) that a studio can self-host against any OpenAI-compatible inference endpoint, so review data never leaves the local infrastructure and the extraction model can be swapped without redesigning the pipeline. The surfaced rules are interpretable: 73.7\% are actionable and each remains traceable to the reviews that support it, so they can inform design decisions directly.

\paragraph{Impact on State of the Art.} The work shows that LLM-based review processing and association mining can be made consistent and interpretable enough to feed a forecasting pipeline, but that their quantitative value for 30-day KPI forecasting is not guaranteed and must be validated per game against an internal baseline before being relied upon.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

\subsection{Validation limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Study limitations.} The validation is preliminary in several respects, each to be read together with the corresponding caveat in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}. Its scope is small: every result comes from a single game, Cyberpunk 2077, over a single year, and both RQ1 and RQ3 explicitly read their answers as possibly game-dependent, so no other game was tested. Moreover, RQ1 measured consistency across models, not correctness: models sharing a systematic bias would agree while being jointly wrong, a threat currently mitigated only by the anti-bias anchoring of the extraction prompt.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.} The interpretation of the results is bounded for related reasons. Following the interpretation safeguards of the methodology, gain-based feature importance is read as descriptive evidence that the regressor leans on a feature, not as evidence that the feature drives player counts. The model coverage is narrow: only four locally hosted, quantized models were benchmarked, no frontier or fine-tuned model was run at production scale, and the labels consumed by the downstream stages come from a single model selected by cross-model consensus. Finally, the ablation outcome may be regime-specific, because the held-out period contains a late-2026 growth episode with player levels beyond almost all training observations, a regime that favors the smoother KPI-only baseline.

\subsection{Future developments}
\label{sec:future_developments}

\paragraph{Improvement path.} Since the pipeline depends on the output of each individual stage and inconsistency compounds downstream, improvements should proceed hierarchically, starting from the review preprocessing and moving downstream stage by stage.

\paragraph{Concrete directions.} The candidate causes identified in Chapter~\ref{chap:results} and summarized in Section~\ref{sec:conclusions_summary} translate into developments that, once more, proceed stage by stage from the mining stage downstream. The forecasting matrix itself should be filtered, not only the displayed rule set: moving the RQ2 decision procedure into the mining filter cascade (e.g., extending entailment rule R2 to the miner itself) would remove the polarity-restatement rules that dominate the residual noise (17.4\% of the inspected sample) and would keep structural rules, such as the hardware pattern ranked ninth by gain in C3, from ever reaching the regressor. Around the rule set, the cross-window de-duplication of the persistence stage should be strengthened, since 36.7\% of the sampled rules duplicate another rule's full item-set signature.

At the forecasting stage, the feature-to-sample ratio needs attention: 31{,}561 candidate rule features against 254 training days could cause variance absorption, so feature selection, regularization or dimensionality reduction should be evaluated, and weekend sparsity, where review-derived features are computed from smaller samples, should be handled explicitly; in parallel, direct multi-horizon training or uncertainty-aware features could limit the variance that compounds over the 30 chained predictions. On the extraction side, frontier or fine-tuned models, possibly fine-tuned on manually annotated aspects, could yield more informative scores, since RQ1 showed larger models to be more consistent. Finally, the whole study should be replicated on multiple games and longer histories, varying lookback windows, to test the model, game and data dependence hypotheses, and the optional manual annotation layer of the inspection protocol should be exercised, reporting its agreement with the rule-based labels.

\paragraph{Alternative approaches.} The fixed extraction schema, and the association mining built on top of it, could be substituted with dynamic feature extraction, using a pipeline inspired by the one Apple has described for the App Store (Section~\ref{sec:sotp}): small, fine-tuned LLMs output descriptive feature phrases from the reviews, and embedding models cluster them. Such a system could extract more specific features than the static schema: instead of knowing that most players have a bad opinion of the game's monetization, it could differentiate between complaints about skins, loot boxes, gacha mechanics and DLC pricing. The trade-off is that the gained features are qualitative instead of quantitative: they cannot be fed directly into the forecasting matrix and would first need to be re-quantified (e.g., as cluster frequencies over time), trading away the fixed quantitative items that made the extraction stage benchmarkable (RQ1) while keeping the traceability through semantic embedding.

\paragraph{A cultural hypothesis.} The full forecaster systematically predicts at or above the actual level: its underestimation rate is 1.77\% against 5.83\% for the KPI-only baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:rq3_forecast}). One may hypothesize that hype-driven, polarized review discourse biases the extracted features towards growth signals, pushing the recursive forecast towards steady growth. This causal link is not tested in this thesis; it is proposed here as a direction for future work, not as a demonstrated claim.

\section{Final Considerations}
\label{sec:final_considerations}
\noindent Overall, the goal stated in Section~\ref{sec:objectives} is met at the design level and only partially at the validation level: StARAPTOR converts unstructured Steam reviews into quantified, traceable inputs for engagement forecasting, and the validation shows that exploiting them for accurate 30-day forecasts remains an open, per-game question. However, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:future_developments}, the barrier may be cultural more than technical.
